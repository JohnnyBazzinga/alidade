# Requirements Document

## Introduction

`nonius` is the application `apps/nonius`: a position fix from the Sun, with no GPS. The user points the phone's camera at the Sun at two or more times of the day, without moving. Each **Sun sight** is the Sun's apparent altitude above the horizontal, measured against gravity by the phone, with the UTC time of the sight and its 1-sigma. At that time the Sun stands overhead at one point of the Earth, and every place that sees it at the measured altitude lies on one circle around that point. Two or more circles cross where the observer is. Core's `solve` computes that crossing with a new **altitude Measurement model** written in this package, and the app draws it with its 95 % ellipse and states it in words. It is a web app that opens from a link, with no account and no install (`product.md`, principle 3).

Nonius is the dual of Vigia. Vigia has many observers at known places and one unknown target; Nonius has one observer at an unknown place and targets at known places in the sky. It uses the skeleton as it is, with one change to UI (`DeviceSighting` takes the kind of capture, skeleton-ui task group 32): `@alidade/core` (angles, positions, the Local plane, `solve`, the Studentised residuals, the ellipse), `@alidade/sensors` (the altitude capture, the Simulator, the magnetic Declination model) and `@alidade/ui` (the altitude Session and its sighting screen, the map with application layers, the Simulator panel). Nonius adds what only it needs:

- the **Sun wrapper**: the Sun's declination and Greenwich hour angle from the UTC time, from `astronomy-engine` through one module, tested against published values _(lead's decision 1 of 2026-11-20)_;
- the **Index error**: the phone's constant altitude error, measured with Sun sights at a place the phone knows, stored, shown with every Fix and removed by the user _(lead's decision 2 of 2026-11-20)_;
- the **Sight**: an altitude capture corrected for the Index error and for refraction, with its error split into the part that is new at each sight and the part that every sight of one phone shares;
- the **altitude model** for Core's solver, the **choice between the two crossings** of two circles, and the **Fix** with an ellipse that carries the shared error;
- the **words**: the Fix, what the sights can and cannot check, the Geometry class and the **advice in time**;
- the **comparison with GPS**, after the Fix and only on the user's act;
- the **reviewer's path**: the whole flow on a laptop, from synthetic scenes.

**Order of the work.** _(Lead's decision 7 of 2026-11-20.)_ The first release is Requirements 1 to 9, 11 to 15, and of Requirement 10 the Geometry class, the simple advice and the rule of thumb (10.1, 10.2, 10.7). The search for the best time over the next 24 hours (Requirement 10.3 to 10.6) is the last task group and may be cut. The work stops twice for the lead's review: after the mathematics (the Sun wrapper, the model, the solve with its covariance, the statistical tests) and at the end.

The central risk is the same as Vigia's: a confident-looking wrong position (`product.md`, principle 1). Three facts of this problem run through the document. First, a degree of altitude is 111 km on the ground, and a phone's altitude is good to a degree at best, so every Fix is hundreds of kilometres wide and says so. Second, most of a phone's altitude error is a bias that every sight of the same phone shares (the sensor probe of 2026-11-15 measured 3.3° ± 0.5° against the Sun on an iPhone): it is a sextant's index error, which the app measures and removes (Requirement 15), and what remains of it does not average away with more sights, so the ellipse carries it (Requirement 7.2). Third, two circles always cross twice and two sights cannot check each other: the app says which crossing it took and why, or shows both and lets the user choose.

Out of scope, as decided by the lead: stars, planets and the Moon; a sea horizon and dip; a moving observer (a run between sights); sight reduction tables; an almanac beyond the Sun; any network request other than map tiles; sharing between phones; accounts; automatic detection of the Sun's glare in the image (UI's Open question 3 left it to this specification: it stays out, because the camera's exposure on the Sun is not measured and the crosshair works without it); landscape layouts; languages other than English. Nonius is not certified navigation equipment and is not to be navigated by alone (`product.md`, non-goals).

The steering stands as written _(lead's decisions 1 and 2 of 2026-11-20)_: the Sun's position comes from `astronomy-engine` (`tech.md`), and absolute altitude is relied on only through a measured Index error, or with the skeleton's uncalibrated sigma when there is none (`math-conventions.md`, the open item of the probe). The private folder `notes/` is not read. Every scene and position in this document and its tests is synthetic, except the published astronomical values of Requirements 3.4 and 4.10.

## Glossary

- **Nonius**: the application specified here. **Core**, **Sensors** and **UI** are `@alidade/core`, `@alidade/sensors` and `@alidade/ui`. **Result**, **Reason**, **Warning**, **Position**, **Supported position**, **Local plane**, **Measurement model**, **Fix**, **Ellipse**, **Effective sigma**, **Normalised residual**, **Studentised residual**, **Cost** and **Crossing angle** are as defined in the sighting-core requirements. **Sensor source**, **Simulator source**, **Altitude calibration**, **Camera altitude**, **Camera azimuth**, **North reference**, **Yaw reference**, **Declination**, **Position fix** and **Clock** are as defined in the skeleton-sensors requirements. **Session**, **Frozen window**, **Mark**, **Message**, **UI reason**, **Camera calibration**, **Synthetic scene**, **Station**, **Tile source**, **Simulator panel** and **Timer** are as defined in the skeleton-ui requirements.
- **Declination** keeps its Sensors meaning: the angle from true north to magnetic north. The Sun's angle from the celestial equator is always called the **Sun's declination** and named `sunDeclinationRad`; no name in Nonius uses a bare `declination` for it.
- **Math layer**, **Components layer**, **Adapter**: as in Vigia. Nonius has one Adapter, the **Storage adapter**.
- **Sun wrapper**: the one module of the Math layer that imports `astronomy-engine` and gives, for a UTC time, the Sun's declination and its **Greenwich hour angle** (GHA, in `[0, 2π)`, westward from the Greenwich meridian) (Requirement 3). _(Lead's decision 1 of 2026-11-20; it was the "Sun model".)_
- **Subsolar point**: the place where the Sun is overhead at a time: `{ latRad: sunDeclinationRad, lonRad: wrapPi(-ghaRad) }` (`math-conventions.md`, Celestial).
- **Apparent altitude**, **geometric altitude**: as in `math-conventions.md`. **Refraction**: apparent minus geometric altitude, from the formula of Requirement 4.7.
- **Index error**: the constant part of the phone's altitude error, measured as the mean of measured minus computed apparent altitude of the Sun at a known place, with its 1-sigma and the time it was measured (Requirement 15). **Calibration in use**: the Index error when there is one; otherwise none, and the skeleton's `UNCALIBRATED_ALTITUDE`. _(Lead's decision 2 of 2026-11-20.)_
- **Sight**: one Sun sight as Nonius holds it (Requirement 4.5): an id, a time, the raw apparent altitude as captured with no calibration, the sample standard deviation and the Mark's term of the capture, a **Sun side** or none, and whether it was simulated. Its **corrected altitude** is the raw altitude minus the Index error of the Calibration in use. **Independent sigma**: the 1-sigma of the part of a sight's altitude error that is new at each sight (the hand's shake, the aim, the tap, the refraction). **Common sigma**: the 1-sigma of the part that is the same for every sight of the set: the Index error's sigma, or `UNCALIBRATED_ALTITUDE.sigmaRad` without one.
- **Sun side**: the Camera azimuth at a sight, with its North reference (`magnetic` or `true`), recorded only to choose between two crossings and never given to the solver (Requirement 6.5).
- **Sight set**: the Sights the user takes to be from one place. There is one Sight set at a time. **Used**, **set aside**: as in Vigia; a Sight is set aside by Core's outlier rejection, or because its corrected altitude falls outside the limits (Requirement 4.8).
- **Circle**: the circle of equal altitude of one Sight: the places whose geometric altitude of the Sun at the Sight's time equals the Sight's corrected altitude less its refraction; centred on the Subsolar point, with a radius of `π/2 - geometric altitude` of arc.
- **Angle of cut**: the acute angle between two Circles where they cross: `min(|Δ|, π - |Δ|)`, where `Δ` is `wrapPi` of the difference of the Sun's true azimuths at that place at the two times (Core's Crossing angle formula). The two crossings of a pair of Circles have the same Angle of cut.
- **Candidate**: one of the two places where the Circles of a pair of Sights cross (Requirement 6.2).
- **Check solve**, **Fix solve**: the two solves of one Candidate (Requirement 7.1): the check solve weighs each Sight by the `hypot` of its Independent and Common sigmas and runs Core's outlier rejection; the Fix solve weighs it by its Independent sigma, on the Sights the check solve kept. _(Lead's decision 4 of 2026-11-20.)_
- **Shown Fix**: the Fix that the app shows: the Fix solve's Fix with the check solve's exclusions and warnings, its covariance replaced by the covariance of Requirement 7.2 and its ellipse recomputed from it.
- **Two places**: the state in which two Candidates' Fixes remain and nothing in the sights decides between them; both are shown and the user chooses (Requirement 6.7). _(Lead's decision 3 of 2026-11-20.)_
- **Geometry class**: `none`, `poor`, `fair` or `good` (Requirement 10.1). **Elongation**: semi-major over semi-minor axis of the Shown Fix's ellipse.
- **Advice in time**: how long to wait for the next sight, and, in the last task group, when a further sight would shrink the 95 % ellipse most (Requirement 10).
- **Comparison**: the distance from the Shown Fix to the phone's GPS position, asked once on the user's act (Requirement 11).
- **Simulation mode**, **Reviewer**: as in Vigia (Requirement 13). **Sight time** of a scene: a simulated time at which a scene expects a sight.
- **Nonius reason**: a member of the closed set of Requirement 2.2. **Nonius result**: a Result whose failure carries a Nonius reason.

## Requirements

### Requirement 1: Package boundaries, layers and rules

**User Story:** As the project's maintainer, I want Nonius to sit on the skeleton's public API alone, with the same rules as Vigia, so that the skeleton stays lean and the two apps stay independent.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE Nonius package SHALL import `@alidade/core`, `@alidade/sensors` and `@alidade/ui` only through their `src/index.ts`, SHALL NOT import from `apps/vigia`, and SHALL import no package other than these, `react`, `react-dom`, `maplibre-gl` (only for its stylesheet) and `astronomy-engine`. _(Lead's decision 1 of 2026-11-20.)_ `astronomy-engine` SHALL be pinned at exactly `2.1.19` (MIT, no dependencies of its own, published 2023-12-14) and imported by one module of the Math layer only, the Sun wrapper. The refraction formula and the crossing of two Circles each take under 30 lines and add no dependency (`tech.md`). A test SHALL scan the imports and `package.json`, whose other versions SHALL be pinned and equal to Vigia's.
2. THE Math layer (the Sun wrapper, refraction, Sights, the Index error, the altitude model, Candidates, the Fix, checks, words, Geometry class, advice, parameters, scenes) SHALL import no React, no browser global and no Adapter, SHALL read no clock and draw no random number, and SHALL receive every time and size as an argument. A test SHALL run its tests in a Node environment with no DOM and SHALL scan its imports.
3. THE Storage adapter SHALL take the storage object it wraps as an injected argument and SHALL be the only module that references `localStorage` or `indexedDB`. NO module SHALL reference `fetch`, `XMLHttpRequest`, `WebSocket`, `EventSource`, `navigator.sendBeacon`, `navigator.share` or `navigator.clipboard`, nor insert an element whose `src` or `href` is a remote URL: Nonius makes no request of its own. A test SHALL scan the source for these names.
4. THE Nonius package SHALL NOT reference `DeviceOrientationEvent` (except the feature test of Requirement 13.1, by `typeof` only), `navigator.geolocation`, `navigator.permissions`, `screen.orientation`, `visibilitychange` or `getUserMedia`: sensors, including the GPS position, belong to Sensors and the camera to UI (`tech.md`). The only module that calls a Sensor source's `startPosition` SHALL be the one that asks the phone's location for the Comparison (Requirement 11) and for the Index error (Requirement 15). _(Lead's decision 2 of 2026-11-20.)_
5. THE Nonius package SHALL read time only from an injected Clock and wait only through an injected Timer, except in the one module that provides their defaults.
6. NOTHING that only Nonius needs SHALL be added to `skeleton/` (`product.md`, principle 5). The one change to the skeleton is skeleton-ui task group 32 (`DeviceSighting` takes the kind of capture). WHEN Nonius needs something else general that the skeleton lacks, THE work SHALL stop for the lead, and SHALL NOT copy skeleton logic into the app (a rotation, a Sighting window, the 95 % scale, the ellipse polygon, the Local plane, the summary of azimuths).
7. EVERY number SHALL carry its unit suffix (`math-conventions.md`); degrees SHALL appear only at the display, in fixtures, in the Sun wrapper's boundary with the library and in parameters' documentation, converted by Core's `degToRad` and `radToDeg` in one module.
8. THE package SHALL build and pass its tests under TypeScript `strict`, with no `any`, and with no non-null assertion in the Math layer.
9. ALL positions SHALL be `{ latRad, lonRad }` on Core's sphere; a position that fails Core's `isSupportedPosition` (within 1° of a pole) SHALL never be shown as a Fix or a Candidate.
10. THE screens SHALL follow Vigia's patterns exactly: UI's palette, sizes and font through a `look.ts`, text of at least 16 CSS pixels, the 7 : 1 contrast of UI Requirement 13.2, everything in the page's normal flow from 320 to 430 CSS pixels wide, portrait only, the primary-action row where the thumb is, UI's `FailureNotice`, one store, and in Simulation mode the phone bar under the banner, which holds the scene's Sight time. _(Lead's decision 7 of 2026-11-20.)_
11. THE Math layer SHALL be covered by a property test for every FOR ALL criterion of this document, named with the criterion in a comment above it, with at least `200` cases, generators that state their domain, and an example test of each degenerate case a generator excludes (`testing.md`). THE Components layer SHALL be covered by example tests in a simulated DOM against the Simulator source, a recording fake Sensor source, a fake Timer, fake Storage and a fake map. Tests SHALL use no real network, camera, clock or randomness, and `pnpm test` SHALL run once and exit.

### Requirement 2: Failures and reasons

**User Story:** As a user on a boat or in a classroom, I want every failure explained in plain words with what to do, so that a code or a blank screen never leaves me guessing.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE Math layer and the Storage adapter SHALL NOT throw, and SHALL NOT return or store `NaN` or an infinite number, for any input that passes type checking. A caught exception from a browser object or from `astronomy-engine` SHALL become a failure value.
2. THE package SHALL define a closed set of failure reasons, `NoniusReason`: every `UiReason` of UI, unchanged in meaning, and exactly these 11: `sun_too_low` (a corrected apparent altitude below the minimum of Requirement 14), `sun_too_high` (above the maximum), `sight_time_unsupported` (a time outside the Sun wrapper's range), `sights_full`, `circles_do_not_meet` (no pair of Circles crosses), `fix_too_uncertain` (Requirement 7.5), `outlier` (the cause of a Sight that Core set aside), `index_error_implausible` (Requirement 15.4), `storage_unavailable`, `storage_corrupt` and `storage_version_unsupported`. Neither Core, Sensors nor UI SHALL be changed for them. _(Lead's decisions 2 and 3 of 2026-11-20: `two_places_fit` is gone, since two places are shown and not refused, and `index_error_implausible` is added.)_
3. EVERY failure SHALL be shown through one function, `messageFor`, whose table is keyed by `NoniusReason` (type `Record<NoniusReason, …>`), and which returns UI's Message for the reasons UI knows except those that `solve` returns for Sights (`insufficient_observations`, `singular_geometry`, `not_converged`, `unsupported_position`, `invalid_observation`, `out_of_range`), for which it SHALL give Nonius's own words about sights and the Sun. THE tests SHALL check that no reason has an empty part and that no part contains a code, an underscore, `NaN`, `Infinity`, `undefined`, `null` or `[object`.
4. THE Message of each Nonius reason SHALL name what the user can act on (how high the Sun must be, how long to wait, the phone's clock, the index error, the storage of the browser) and SHALL NOT say only "try again".
5. THE app SHALL NOT drop a failure: each SHALL appear on the screen, in the cause of a set-aside Sight, or in the value an Adapter returns.
6. FOR ALL type-correct inputs drawn by each public Math-layer function's generator, including `NaN`, `±Infinity`, `-0`, empty and very long strings and the doubles on either side of every limit, the function SHALL return without throwing, and every number reachable in its result SHALL be finite. A registry SHALL list every public Math-layer function with its generator, and a completeness test SHALL fail when one is missing (Vigia's pattern).
7. THE altitude model SHALL NOT return Core's `diverging_bearings` or `colocated_observers`, whose meanings are those of bearings, and SHALL return `out_of_range` only for a candidate more than `PLANE_LIMIT_M` from its origin (Requirement 5.11); a test SHALL search its source and its results for them. _(Lead's decision 5 of 2026-11-20.)_

### Requirement 3: Where the Sun is

_(Rewritten by the lead's decision 1 of 2026-11-20: the Sun's position comes from `astronomy-engine`, as `tech.md` says, through one wrapper; the errors that matter are the wrapper's (time scales, frames, units), so the wrapper is what is tested.)_

**User Story:** As a navigation student, I want the app to know where the Sun is from the time alone, to a stated accuracy checked against published values, so that the Sun's position is never the weak part of the fix.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE Math layer SHALL provide the Sun wrapper, `sunPosition(timeUtcMs)`, returning `sunDeclinationRad` and `ghaRad` (in `[0, 2π)`), and `subsolarPoint(timeUtcMs)`, returning the Subsolar point. It SHALL take the Sun's geocentric apparent position (with aberration) from `astronomy-engine`, referred to the true equator and equinox of date, and the Greenwich apparent sidereal time from the same library, with `GHA = wrapTwoPi(GAST - right ascension)`. It SHALL give the library the time as Universal Time in J2000 days, computed from `timeUtcMs` by arithmetic and not through `Date`, so that UTC stands for UT1 (an error under `0.9 s`, under `0.23′` of GHA); the library's own model of TT SHALL be used. Nothing else in the package SHALL import the library, and the wrapper SHALL use none of its refraction or horizon functions (Bennett's formula of Requirement 4.7 is the app's).
2. THE wrapper SHALL accept times from `SUN_MODEL_START_UTC_MS` (2000-01-01T00:00Z) up to, not including, `SUN_MODEL_END_UTC_MS` (2050-01-01T00:00Z), and SHALL return `sight_time_unsupported` outside them, so that a phone whose clock has been reset (to 1970, or to 1980 for a GPS epoch) is refused rather than given a Fix thousands of kilometres off; the Message SHALL name the phone's clock. A failure or a non-finite number from the library SHALL become `sight_time_unsupported` naming the field.
3. THE wrapper SHALL be a pure function of its argument: no table, no network, no clock.
4. THE wrapper SHALL be tested against published values kept as a read-only fixture in `fixtures/`, each row with its source (book, edition and page, or address and retrieval date): Meeus, _Astronomical Algorithms_, 2nd edition, Example 25.a (the Sun's apparent declination and right ascension on 1992 October 13.0 TD, its time taken as UTC) and Examples 12.a and 12.b (sidereal time); and at least `12` values of the Sun's GHA and declination tabulated in The Nautical Almanac, or in the USNO's celestial navigation data, one in each month, spread over at least two years between 2000 and 2049. These are the only fixtures of the package that are not synthetic.
5. AT every row of the fixture, THE Sun's declination SHALL differ from the published value by at most `1.0′`, and the GHA (or, for Example 25.a, the right ascension) by at most `1.5′` as a wrapped difference; sidereal time SHALL differ from Examples 12.a and 12.b by at most `0.1 s` plus the equation of the equinoxes where the example gives mean time. The bounds are those first proposed for a hand-written formula, kept by the lead's decision because they hold the wrapper to account for its time scales and frames: `1′` of position, `0.23′` for UT1, `0.05′` for the rounding of the tables to `0.1′`, with margin. A `1.5′` error moves a Fix by under `3 km`, under 3 % of the `111 km` of one degree of the phone's altitude. A row outside a bound SHALL stop the work and be classified (`testing.md`, rule 1); the bound SHALL NOT be widened to pass.
6. FOR ALL times in the range, `|sunDeclinationRad|` SHALL be at most `23.45°`, `ghaRad` SHALL lie in `[0, 2π)`, and `wrapPi(ghaRad - (15° per hour of UTC since 0h - 180°))` (the equation of time) SHALL be within `±4.5°` (the equation of time stays within about `±16.5` minutes, `±4.1°`).
7. FOR ALL times `t` and durations `Δ` in `[1 min, 6 h]` with `t + Δ` in the range, the GHA SHALL advance by `15°` per hour of `Δ` within `0.006°` per hour (the equation of time changes by at most about `30 s` a day), and the Sun's declination SHALL change by at most `0.017°` per hour of `Δ` (its largest rate is about `0.4°` a day, at the equinoxes).
8. THE design SHALL report what the library adds to the built bundle, minified and compressed, for the functions the wrapper imports.

### Requirement 4: Taking a Sun sight

**User Story:** As a sailor, I want to point the phone at the Sun on the screen and get its altitude with an honest uncertainty, without ever looking at the Sun.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE sighting screen SHALL be UI's `SightingScreen` driven by an `altitude` Session: on a phone through UI's `DeviceSighting` with the kind `altitude` (skeleton-ui task group 32), and in Simulation mode on the Simulator source. Nonius SHALL NOT reimplement any part of the capture. Because UI's altitude Session asks for no position, THE app SHALL ask the phone for no location while sighting; a component test with a recording Sensor source SHALL show that `startPosition` is never called by the sighting screen.
2. THE sighting screen SHALL say, before the first sight of a Sight set and in a form that stays reachable: look only at the screen, never at the Sun, and never through binoculars or any lens; put the crosshair on the centre of the Sun's glare; hold still; and stay in one place for all the sights of a set.
3. ON a phone, THE app SHALL pass UI no Camera calibration, so that the sight is taken along the crosshair and a tap is refused by the skeleton (`calibration_missing`), as Vigia does. IN Simulation mode, THE app SHALL pass the scene's Camera calibration and `SIMULATION_TAP_SIGMA_PX`, so that the Reviewer can freeze, mark the Sun and capture, and the screen SHALL say that these are simulation values.
4. THE Altitude calibration given to every capture SHALL be `{ offsetRad: 0, sigmaRad: 0 }`, so that the capture is raw; the Index error and the Common sigma SHALL be applied when the Fix is computed (Requirement 7.1), so that measuring or removing an Index error applies to every Sight of the set at once. THE sighting screen SHALL show the Calibration in use: the Index error with its sigma and date, or that the phone was never calibrated. _(Lead's decision 2 of 2026-11-20.)_
5. WHEN a capture succeeds, THE app SHALL make a Sight of it: an id unique in the Sight set; the time, the middle of the Frozen window (`frozenAtUtcMs - windowLengthMs / 2`, because the altitude is the mean over the window; the Sun's altitude changes by at most `0.25′` a second, so the choice matters by at most `0.13′`); the raw apparent altitude of the capture, unchanged; the capture's sample standard deviation and the Mark's term `ε` of UI Requirement 12.4 (`0` without a Mark); the Sun side (criterion 6); and whether it was simulated. ITS Independent sigma SHALL be `hypot(sample standard deviation, ε, AIM_SIGMA_RAD, refraction sigma)`, with the refraction sigma of criterion 7 at its corrected altitude. _(Lead's decision 2 of 2026-11-20.)_
6. THE Sun side SHALL be the circular mean of the Camera azimuths over the Frozen window, computed with Sensors' exported functions, in the North reference of the Frozen sighting: the Yaw reference's when the Frozen sighting holds a valid one, `magnetic` for an Absolute frame without one, and none for a Relative frame without one or when Sensors returns a failure. A sight SHALL be captured with or without a Sun side; the screen SHALL say whether it has one and, with none, that the compass helps to tell two places apart (Requirement 6.5).
7. THE app SHALL correct the corrected apparent altitude for refraction by Bennett's formula, `R = cot(h + 7.31 / (h + 4.4))` arc-minutes with `h` the apparent altitude in degrees (G. G. Bennett, 1982, as given in Meeus, _Astronomical Algorithms_, chapter 16; stated accuracy `0.07′` from 0° to 90° in standard conditions, 10 °C and 1010 hPa), and SHALL add a refraction sigma of `REFRACTION_SIGMA_FRACTION · R` to the Independent sigma. `astronomy-engine`'s refraction SHALL NOT be used. THE app SHALL apply no dip (the horizontal comes from gravity), no semi-diameter (the crosshair is on the centre), and no parallax (the Sun's is under `0.15′`), and the "how it works" text SHALL say so.
8. IF the corrected apparent altitude is below `MIN_SIGHT_ALTITUDE_RAD` (`5°`), THEN the capture SHALL be refused with `sun_too_low`, whose Message SHALL say how high the Sun must be (about three fingers' width at arm's length above the horizon), that at night or with the Sun lower there is nothing to sight, and that stars are not in this version. IF it is above `MAX_SIGHT_ALTITUDE_RAD` (`80°`), THEN with `sun_too_high`, saying to wait until the Sun is lower. IF the Sight's time is outside the Sun wrapper's range, THEN with `sight_time_unsupported`; IF the Sight set is full, THEN with `sights_full`. WHEN a later change of the Index error moves a stored Sight's corrected altitude outside the limits, THE Sight SHALL be set aside with `sun_too_low` or `sun_too_high` as its cause, and not deleted.
9. THE list of Sights SHALL show for each its corrected apparent altitude with its total 1-sigma (`hypot` of the Independent and Common sigmas) in degrees, its time in UTC and its age, whether it has a Sun side, and whether it was simulated.
10. THE refraction formula SHALL have an example test against published values of the standard refraction (The Nautical Almanac's refraction table, or the values printed in Meeus, chapter 16) at apparent altitudes of `5°`, `10°`, `20°`, `45°` and `80°`, within `0.1′`, kept with the Sun wrapper's fixture.
11. FOR ALL geometric altitudes in `[4°, 80°]`, the apparent altitude given by the inverse of Bennett's formula (solved to `1e-12` rad), corrected by the formula, SHALL return the geometric altitude within `1e-12` rad; and `R` SHALL be positive and decreasing on `[5°, 80°]`.
12. FOR ALL altitude captures with apparent altitudes in `[5°, 80°]`, sample standard deviations in `[0, 5°]` and Marks or none, the Sight SHALL keep the capture's apparent altitude exactly; and FOR ALL Calibrations in use (Index errors in `[-10°, 10°]` with sigmas in `[0, 5°]`, or none), its corrected altitude SHALL be the raw altitude minus the Index error exactly, its Independent sigma SHALL equal the formula of criterion 5 within `1e-12` rad, and its Common sigma SHALL be the Index error's sigma, or `UNCALIBRATED_ALTITUDE.sigmaRad` without one, exactly. _(Lead's decision 2 of 2026-11-20.)_

### Requirement 5: The altitude Measurement model

**User Story:** As the skeleton's maintainer, I want Nonius's altitude model to plug into Core's solver with no change to Core, so that the claim of Core's Requirement 5 is proved by the second app.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE altitude model SHALL be written in the Nonius package as an implementation of Core's `MeasurementModel` interface, and NO file of `skeleton/core` SHALL be changed for it. _(Lead's decision 5 of 2026-11-20.)_
2. THE model SHALL predict a Sight's geometric altitude at a candidate Position from `sin h = sin φ · sin δ + cos φ · cos δ · cos LHA`, with `δ` the Sun's declination, `LHA = GHA + lonRad`, both from the Sun wrapper at the Sight's time (`math-conventions.md`, Celestial), the argument of `asin` clamped only within `1e-12`.
3. THE residual SHALL be the Sight's corrected apparent altitude minus its refraction (Requirement 4.7), minus the predicted altitude. Both lie in `[-π/2, π/2]`, so the residual is its own `wrapPi`.
4. THE Effective sigma SHALL be the Sight's Independent sigma in the Fix solve and the `hypot` of its Independent and Common sigmas in the check solve (Requirement 7.1); it SHALL be greater than zero for every valid Sight. _(Lead's decision 4 of 2026-11-20; the sigma of the check solve is the design's answer to it, shown by Requirement 8.5.)_
5. THE model SHALL take its starting Position as an argument when it is made (Requirement 6.3); its `origin` SHALL return that Position and its `startEstimate` the point `(0, 0)`.
6. THE model's `geometryCheck` SHALL compute the largest Angle of cut over all pairs of Sights at its starting Position, SHALL fail with `singular_geometry` below `0.5°` (the bearing model's limit, Core Requirement 9.3), and SHALL warn `poor_geometry` below Core's exported `POOR_GEOMETRY_CROSSING_RAD` (`20°`).
7. THE model's `validate` SHALL accept a Sight only if its id is non-empty, its time is in the Sun wrapper's range, its corrected apparent altitude is in `[MIN_SIGHT_ALTITUDE_RAD, MAX_SIGHT_ALTITUDE_RAD]`, and its Effective sigma is finite and in `[0.01°, 45°]`; otherwise it SHALL fail with `invalid_observation` naming the id and the field.
8. FOR ALL candidate Positions with latitudes in `[-85°, 85°]` and times in the range at which the Sun's geometric altitude there lies in `[5°, 80°]`, the model's partial derivatives on the Local plane of the candidate itself (at `(0, 0)`) SHALL equal `(sin A, cos A) / R` within a relative `1e-6`, `A` being the Sun's true azimuth there (Core's `initialAzimuthRad` to the Subsolar point) and `R` the sphere's radius of `math-conventions.md`; and on the Local plane of any origin within `PLANE_LIMIT_M`, they SHALL agree with central differences of step `10 m` within a relative `1e-3` or an absolute `1e-12` rad per metre. _(Amended as a consequence of the lead's decision 5 of 2026-11-20: the model's own derivatives are differences of step `1 m`, so the property compares them with the exact gradient at the centre and with a different step elsewhere.)_
9. FOR ALL such Positions and times, a noise-free Sight (its apparent altitude made from the geometric altitude by the inverse of Requirement 4.11, with no Index error) SHALL have a residual at the true Position of magnitude below `1e-9` rad.
10. FOR ALL sets of 2 to 8 noise-free Sights of one true Position (latitude in `[-80°, 80°]`, times in the range spread over at most 24 hours, the Sun's geometric altitude at the Position in `[5°, 80°]` at every time, a largest Angle of cut at the Position of at least `20°`) and starting Positions within `300 km` of the truth, the Nonius solve of Requirement 7.8 with the model SHALL return a Fix within `0.1 m` of the true Position.
11. THE model's `evaluate` SHALL fail with `out_of_range` for a candidate whose distance from its origin on the Local plane exceeds `PLANE_LIMIT_M` (`1 000 km`, the bound of Core's Requirement 3.3), so that Core's solver, which treats a trial point that fails to evaluate as a rise in cost, never evaluates the model beyond the bound in which Core guarantees the Local plane. _(Lead's decision 5 of 2026-11-20.)_

### Requirement 6: The starting point and the choice between two crossings

**User Story:** As a user who does not know where I am, I want the app to find its own starting point and to tell me which of two possible places it chose and why, or to show me both, so that I am never asked for the thing I am trying to find.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE app SHALL NOT use, for the Fix, its starting point or the choice between Candidates: the phone's location, a position stored or typed, a default position, the Tile source or the time zone. The Math-layer functions that compute them SHALL take Sights, the Calibration in use and parameters only and no Position (a type test), and the only Positions they use are those they compute from the Sights. The location asked for the Index error and for the Comparison SHALL never reach them.
2. THE app SHALL compute, for every pair of used Sights, the places where their two Circles cross on the sphere, in closed form: none, one where they touch, or two. It SHALL take the pair with the largest Angle of cut at its crossings (on a tie, the first in the order of the ids), and its two crossings as the Candidates; a crossing that is not a Supported position SHALL be dropped. IF no pair of Circles crosses, THEN the app SHALL fail with `circles_do_not_meet`, whose Message SHALL say that the sights are too close in time for their error, or that one is wrong.
3. THE app SHALL run the check solve of Requirement 7.1 once from each Candidate, with the altitude model made with that Candidate as its starting Position. A Candidate whose check solve fails is out of the choice. IF both check solves give Fixes within `SAME_FIX_DISTANCE_M` of each other, THEN there is one Fix, the one of lower Cost (on a tie, the first Candidate in the order of its latitude, then longitude), and no choice to explain.
4. THE app SHALL never show the Fix of a Candidate that criteria 5 to 7 did not choose, except as one of the Two places of criterion 7, and SHALL never show the position of an unchosen Candidate. _(Lead's decision 3 of 2026-11-20.)_
5. **The Sun side.** FOR EACH remaining Fix and each used Sight with a Sun side, THE app SHALL compute the Sun's true azimuth at the Fix at the Sight's time and, for a `magnetic` Sun side, the Declination at the Fix and time from Sensors' `declinationAt` (a failure of it, such as a time outside the World Magnetic Model's 2025 to 2030, leaves that Sight out of the choice). A Fix SHALL be contradicted when any such Sight's true Sun side differs from the computed azimuth by more than `SUN_SIDE_TOLERANCE_RAD` (a wrapped difference). WHEN exactly one Fix is not contradicted and the other is, THE app SHALL choose it and SHALL say why in compass words ("at the other place, about 3 400 km away, the Sun would have been to the north-east at 10:12 UTC; your phone pointed south-east"). The Sun side SHALL NOT enter the solve.
6. **The fit.** OTHERWISE, WITH three or more used Sights, WHEN the check solve's Cost of one Fix is lower than the other's by at least `CROSSING_COST_MARGIN`, THE app SHALL choose it and SHALL say that only this place fits all the sights and by how much the other misses the worst one, in degrees; WHEN the Sun side contradicted both Fixes, the words SHALL also say that the compass disagreed with both places and was not used.
7. **Two places.** _(Rewritten by the lead's decision 3 of 2026-11-20: the app does not refuse.)_ OTHERWISE, WHEN two Fixes remain, THE app SHALL show both as Two places: each drawn on the map with its 95 % ellipse (Requirement 9.4) and stated in words with "probably within about N km" (Requirement 9.1), with the distance between them, why the sights cannot tell them apart (two sights always cross twice; no usable compass reading, naming the reason: none was taken, or the iPhone's compass does not work with the camera more than `30°` up; or, when the Sun's declination is within `2°` of zero at every used Sight, that near an equinox the Sun alone cannot tell a northern latitude from the southern one), and the words "Two places fit these sights. Choose the one you are near." THE user SHALL choose one by a control for each; the chosen one SHALL become the Shown Fix, its words SHALL begin with "You chose this place of two" and the direction and distance of the other, and one control SHALL undo the choice. THE choice SHALL be held in memory for the used set it was made on, and SHALL be cleared, and the Two places shown again, when that set changes or the page is reloaded. Before the choice, no Geometry class, advice or Comparison SHALL be offered. WHEN one Fix remains (the other check solve failed or its Candidate was dropped), THE app SHALL show it as the Shown Fix and SHALL say that the other crossing gave no solution; IF the Sun side contradicts it, THEN its words SHALL begin with that disagreement (the compass and the sights do not agree; check the compass, or take another sight).
8. THE words of the Shown Fix SHALL carry the choice and its reason; WITH exactly two used Sights they SHALL also say that two sights cannot check each other.
9. FOR ALL sets of 2 to 8 noise-free Sights (the domain of Requirement 5.10, times in 2026 to 2029), with no Index error, Independent sigma `0.5°`, Common sigma `0`, and each Sight's Sun side the Sun's true azimuth at the truth (north reference `true`) or absent, at random, THE app SHALL show either a Shown Fix within `0.1 m` of the true Position, or Two places one of which lies within `0.1 m` of it, and never the other place alone; and whenever two Fixes remain and at least one Sight has a Sun side for which their computed azimuths differ by more than `2 · SUN_SIDE_TOLERANCE_RAD`, it SHALL show the Shown Fix. _(Lead's decision 3 of 2026-11-20.)_
10. FOR ALL sets as in criterion 9 and all permutations of their order, the Candidates, the choice (or the Two places, in the same order) and the Shown Fix SHALL be identical.
11. THE choice SHALL have example tests: an equinox case (four noise-free Sights over six hours on a day when the Sun's declination is within `0.5°` of zero, without a Sun side, gives Two places, one at the true place and the other near the opposite latitude; with one `magnetic` Sun side made with the Declination of Sensors' model it gives the true place); a two-Sight case decided by the Sun side; and a two-Sight case with no Sun side, chosen by the user, undone by the control and chosen again.

### Requirement 7: The Fix and its uncertainty

**User Story:** As a navigator, I want the ellipse to cover where I am at least 95 times in 100, including the error my phone makes the same way at every sight, so that more sights never make the app more confident than it should be.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. _(Lead's decision 4 of 2026-11-20.)_ FOR the chosen Candidate, THE app SHALL run, with the corrected altitudes of the Calibration in use: the **check solve**, Core's `solve` with each Sight's Effective sigma the `hypot` of its Independent and Common sigmas and `outlierRejection: true`; then the **Fix solve**, Core's `solve` from the check solve's Fix, on the Sights the check solve did not exclude, with each Sight's Independent sigma and `outlierRejection: false`. THE Shown Fix SHALL be the Fix solve's Fix with the check solve's `excludedIds` and warnings, its covariance that of criterion 2, and its ellipse Core's `ellipseFromCovariance` of that covariance; it SHALL be drawn by UI's `setFix` alone (Requirement 9.4); and no component SHALL receive the position of a Fix without its ellipse (a type test).
2. _(Lead's decision 4 of 2026-11-20.)_ THE covariance of the Shown Fix SHALL be Core's covariance of the Fix solve plus `σc² · g gᵀ`, where `σc` is the Common sigma and `g`, in East-North metres per radian at the Fix, is the shift of the Fix when every corrected altitude is shifted by the same amount: `g = (F₊ - F₋) / (2 · SENSITIVITY_STEP_RAD)`, with `F₊` and `F₋` the Fix solves (from the Fix) with every corrected altitude shifted by `+SENSITIVITY_STEP_RAD` and `-SENSITIVITY_STEP_RAD`, projected onto the Local plane of the Fix with Core's `projectToLocalPlane`. The reason: a shared error `b` moves every Circle by the same amount, so the Fix moves by `g · b`, which does not shrink as Sights are added; if all of the error were shared, an ellipse that took it as independent would be too small along the bisector of the Sun's azimuths by a factor of about `1.4` with two Sights whose Angle of cut is `90°`, and of about `2.4` with six whose azimuths are spread evenly over `120°`.
3. FOR ALL runs of a statistical test with a seed passed as an argument and at least `2 000` runs in each band of the Common sigma, `0`, `0.25°`, `1°` and `3.5°`: true Positions with latitudes in `[-70°, 70°]`, times in 2026 to 2029, 2 to 6 Sights spread over 2 to 9 hours with the Sun at the Position in `[5°, 80°]` at each, Independent sigma `0.5°`, one shared error per run drawn from a normal of the Common sigma, one independent error per Sight drawn from a normal of the Independent sigma, and a `true` Sun side with normal noise of `5°`, with the true place chosen when the run ends in Two places: THE true Position SHALL lie inside the 95 % ellipse of the Shown Fix in at least `90 %` of the runs whose Fix is shown and whose Geometry class is not `poor`, in every band; and in at most `99 %` of them in the band with no Common sigma. In the other bands the upper share is reported and not bounded, because an ellipse that is too large is the lesser fault here. _(Lead's decision 4 of 2026-11-20.)_ The runs with a `poor` Fix are reported and not bounded (the words say the position could be far along the long axis, Requirement 10.1); the runs that end in each failure reason, and in Two places, are counted and reported; a band with fewer than `100` bounded runs is reported and not bounded. A result outside the bounds SHALL stop the work and be classified (`testing.md`, rule 1).
4. THE app SHALL recompute the Shown Fix whenever the used set or the Calibration in use changes, within one screen update, and SHALL clear an earlier Fix when the new one fails, as Vigia does.
5. IF the 95 % semi-major axis of the Shown Fix exceeds `MAX_FIX_SEMI_MAJOR_95_M` (`2 000 km`), THEN the app SHALL show no position and fail with `fix_too_uncertain`, whose Message SHALL give the size, say why (the phone's altitude sigma, with or without an Index error, and the Angle of cut) and what helps (measuring the index error, more sights hours apart).
6. WHEN a solve fails, THE app SHALL show the failure through `messageFor` in words about Sights: `insufficient_observations` (fewer than two used Sights), `singular_geometry` ("the Sun's direction has changed too little between sights", with the advice of Requirement 10.7), `not_converged`, `out_of_range` (the sights lead more than 1 000 km from where their Circles cross, so at least one is wrong), `unsupported_position` (the Fix would be within 1° of a pole, where the skeleton does not work) and `invalid_observation`.
7. FOR ALL Shown Fixes, the 95 % ellipse SHALL be Core's `ellipse95` of the Shown Fix's ellipse, and the covariance SHALL be symmetric and positive definite.
8. _(Added by the lead's decision 5 of 2026-11-20.)_ EVERY solve of Nonius SHALL be a re-centred solve: Core's `solve` from the starting point, then again with the model made at the Fix it returned, until a Fix lies within `RECENTRE_TOLERANCE_M` of the one before, at most `MAX_RECENTRE_ROUNDS` calls; IF the rounds run out, THEN it SHALL fail with `not_converged`. By Requirement 5.11, no call evaluates the model beyond `PLANE_LIMIT_M` from its origin; a call whose solution lies beyond fails with `out_of_range`, and the Nonius solve returns that failure.
9. _(Added by the lead's decision 4 of 2026-11-20.)_ FOR ALL linear test models (a test-only Measurement model whose predictions are linear in East and North, with 2 to 8 observations, random rows, Independent sigmas in `[0.1°, 2°]` and Common sigmas in `[0, 3.5°]`), the covariance of criterion 2 SHALL equal `(JᵀWJ)⁻¹ + σc² (A·1)(A·1)ᵀ`, with `A = (JᵀWJ)⁻¹JᵀW` and `W` the inverse squares of the Independent sigmas, within a relative `1e-6` in each entry; this is the covariance of the weighted least-squares Fix when the errors are independent plus one shared, and the property is the method's proof.

### Requirement 8: What the sights can check

**User Story:** As a navigation teacher, I want the app to say what a set of sights can and cannot check, so that my students learn why a third sight matters.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE words SHALL state, at each count of used Sights, what can be checked: with one, there is a Circle and no position; with two, the Circles cross twice and "two sights cannot check each other: a wrong one gives a wrong place and nothing on the screen can tell"; with three, they can be seen to disagree, but not which one is off; with four or more, Core sets aside a Sight whose Studentised residual exceeds `OUTLIER_STUDENTISED_RESIDUAL` (Core's export, `3.5`), a statistical rule that can keep a wrong Sight or set aside a good one, more often with four or five.
2. WITH exactly three used Sights, WHEN a Studentised residual of the chosen Candidate's check solve exceeds Core's threshold, THE words of the Fix SHALL begin with it: the sights do not agree, at least one is off (a reflection, the wrong light, a moved phone, a wrong clock), three cannot tell which and a fourth can; SHALL give the position as the best fit of sights that disagree and not as "probably within"; and SHALL say that the 95 % area may not contain the observer. The map SHALL still draw the Fix with its ellipse.
3. WITH fewer than four used Sights, THE app SHALL NOT name a Sight as the cause, SHALL NOT mark one as disagreeing, and SHALL NOT show a Fix computed without one.
4. WITH four or more, A Sight in the check solve's `excludedIds` SHALL be shown as set aside with the cause `outlier`, and its Circle drawn apart from the used ones (Requirement 9.4). THE app SHALL apply no rejection rule of its own and SHALL drop no Sight by itself; the user may delete a Sight (Requirement 12.3).
5. _(Lead's decision 4 of 2026-11-20.)_ THE test of disagreement and the outlier rejection SHALL be those of the check solve, whose Effective sigma is the `hypot` of the Independent and Common sigmas: the shared error then enters the sigma that the residuals are judged by, instead of appearing as disagreement, as it would with the Independent sigma alone (with three Sights whose azimuths are `60°` apart, a shared error of `3.5°` would leave each residual at about `1.2°` against a sigma of `0.29°` for its part, a Studentised residual of about 4 in every set). FOR ALL sets of the statistical test of Requirement 7.3 with 3 to 6 Sights and no wrong one, the share of sets in which the app says that the sights disagree or sets one aside SHALL be at most `2 %` in each band of the Common sigma (Core Requirement 10.11 uses the same share). A share above it SHALL stop the work and be classified.
6. FOR ALL sets as in criterion 5 with one Sight's altitude wrong by `3°` to `20°`, THE test SHALL report, by size of error and by count, the shares in which the disagreement was said, the wrong Sight alone was set aside, a good one was set aside, and each failure reason; these describe Core's rule and are not bounded.

### Requirement 10: Geometry and advice in time

_(Restructured by the lead's decision 7 of 2026-11-20: the Geometry class, the simple advice and the rule of thumb are in the first release; the search for the best time, criteria 3 to 6, is the last task group and may be cut.)_

**User Story:** As a user with two sights taken close together, I want to be told why the fix is poor and how long to wait for the next sight, so that I know what to do next.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE app SHALL give the Shown Fix a Geometry class, a pure function of Core's warnings and the Shown Fix's ellipse alone: `poor` with Core's `poor_geometry` or an Elongation of at least `LONG_ELLIPSE_RATIO`; `fair` with no warning and an Elongation from `FAIR_ELLIPSE_RATIO` up to `LONG_ELLIPSE_RATIO`; `good` below `FAIR_ELLIPSE_RATIO`; `none` with no Fix. 5. THE hypothetical Sights SHALL exist only inside the advice: never shown, drawn or stored, and the advice SHALL use no formula of its own for the ellipse.
6. FOR ALL Shown Fixes from the sets of Requirement 7.3, the best time SHALL lie on the grid of criterion 3, within the horizon, at a Sun's altitude at the Fix within the limits, and its area SHALL be at most the area at every other time of the grid.
7. WHEN there is no Shown Fix (no Sight, one Sight, Two places not yet chosen, or `singular_geometry`, `circles_do_not_meet`, `fix_too_uncertain`), THE app SHALL give the rule of thumb in words: the Sun's direction must change by at least 20° between sights; away from midday at middle latitudes it turns by about 8° to 16° an hour, so wait about `FIRST_WAIT_MS` (2 hours) after the latest sight; a morning sight and an afternoon sight usually cross well. An example test SHALL check the sentence; a counter test over latitudes from `60° S` to `60° N` and one date in each month SHALL report the share of first sights after which waiting `FIRST_WAIT_MS` gives an Angle of cut of at least 20°, and does not bound it.

### Requirement 12: The sights on the phone

### Requirement 15: Index error

_(Added by the lead's decision 2 of 2026-11-20.)_

**User Story:** As a user of a phone whose altitude reads a few degrees off, I want to measure that constant error once at a place the phone knows and have every fix corrected for it, so that the fix is hundreds of kilometres better and says how it was corrected.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE app SHALL offer a screen "Measure the index error", reachable from the Fix and the Sights screens, which says what it does: at a place this phone knows, a few Sun sights are compared with the altitude the Sun has there and then, and the constant difference is the phone's index error, as on a sextant.
2. ONLY on the user's act on that screen, THE app SHALL ask the phone's location once, for this purpose alone, through the module of Requirement 1.4, with the Coarse location limit `LOCATION_COARSE_LIMIT_M`, and SHALL stop that Sensor source after its first fix or failure. THE screen SHALL show the location's accuracy, and SHALL say that the location is used only to compute the Sun's altitude for the index error, is never used for a Fix, and is not kept. A failure SHALL be shown through `messageFor`.
3. WITH the location known, THE user SHALL take one or more Sun sights on the same altitude Session as Requirement 4 (raw, Requirement 4.4, within the limits of 4.8 applied to the raw altitude). FOR EACH, THE app SHALL compute the Sun's apparent altitude at the location and the sight's time (the Sun wrapper's geometric altitude made apparent by the inverse of Requirement 4.11) and the difference `dₖ = measured − computed`. THE Index error SHALL be the mean of the `dₖ` of the `n` sights, and its sigma `sqrt(s² / n + (σpos / R)² + SUN_ALTITUDE_SIGMA_RAD²)`, with `s` the larger of the sample standard deviation of the `dₖ` (for `n ≥ 2`) and the root mean square of the sights' Independent sigmas, `σpos` the 1-sigma accuracy of the location in metres and `R` the sphere's radius. THE screen SHALL show each `dₖ`, the mean and the sigma as they are taken.
4. IF the magnitude of the Index error exceeds `MAX_INDEX_ERROR_RAD`, THEN the app SHALL refuse to save it with `index_error_implausible`, whose Message SHALL say that a sight was probably not of the Sun, or that the phone's clock or location is wrong, and to take the sights again.
5. ON the user's act "Save", THE app SHALL store the Index error, its sigma, the number of sights and the time of the last sight (Requirement 12.2), replacing any earlier one, and SHALL drop the location and the calibration sights, which never enter the Sight set. THE user SHALL be able to remove the Index error by one control, after one confirmation.
6. WHILE an Index error is stored, EVERY Fix SHALL use it (Requirements 4.4, 7.1) and SHALL show it (Requirement 9.3); WHEN it is older than `INDEX_ERROR_OLD_MS`, the words SHALL give its age and say that it should be measured again. WITHOUT one, the Fix SHALL use `UNCALIBRATED_ALTITUDE` and the words of Requirement 9.3.
7. IN Simulation mode, THE place the phone knows SHALL be the scene's true position, labelled as such, with the accuracy `SIMULATION_LOCATION_ACCURACY_M` (`5 m`, the accuracy of the Position fix in the scripts of UI's catalogue), and no Sensor source SHALL be asked for a position; the scenes' own Index error records SHALL be marked simulated and stored only with the simulation's data (Requirement 12.1).
8. FOR ALL true Positions with latitudes in `[-80°, 80°]`, times in 2026 to 2029 at which the Sun there lies within the limits, biases `b` in `[-10°, 10°]` (at least `1e-6` inside the limit of criterion 4) and sets of 1 to 10 noise-free sights made by adding `b` to the true apparent altitude, the Index error SHALL equal `b` within `1e-9` rad; its sigma SHALL equal the formula of criterion 3 within `1e-12` rad and be at least `SUN_ALTITUDE_SIGMA_RAD`; and the Fix of noise-free Sights of the same Position with the same bias, corrected by that Index error, SHALL lie within `0.1 m` of the true Position.
9. A component test with a recording Sensor source SHALL show exactly one `startPosition` per act of criterion 2 and none otherwise, and that after Save the stored data and the store's state hold no Position.

## Device checks

These can be confirmed only on phones, outdoors. None of the criteria above depends on their outcome, but each, once measured, may change a default or a Requirement. Results that name real places stay in the private `notes/`; only aggregate numbers come into the repository.

D1. **The index error.** At a place whose position is known from the GPS, measure the index error with the app's own screen (Requirement 15) on the iPhone 13 and the Xiaomi, by the crosshair, with 5 sights each, three times over a day and again a week later. Repeat once with the phone turned upside down (rotated 180° about the camera axis), so that an offset of the camera's axis changes sign and one of the sensor does not. Record the index error, its scatter, its change over the day and the week, and which of the two causes it is. This settles the open item of `math-conventions.md`, `AIM_SIGMA_RAD` and `INDEX_ERROR_OLD_MS`.
D2. **Aiming at the glare.** Check on both phones that the Sun's glare on the screen is centred on the disc (by comparing a sight on the glare with one taken through a solar filter held over the camera's lens), that the screen can be read in full sun, and that the camera keeps working after a minute pointed at the Sun.
D3. **Whole fixes.** Take at least 10 sets of three sights, hours apart, on several days, with the index error measured on another day at another place, and the Comparison after each; record the share of Fixes whose 95 % ellipse holds the GPS position. If it is far from 95 %, the sigma of the sights is wrong and Requirement 9.2's statement is revised before a demo.
D4. **The Sun side.** On both phones, compare the Sun side of each sight (after Declination) with the Sun's true azimuth at the GPS position, at Sun altitudes from 5° to 70°, and on the iPhone with north set from the compass with the phone upright and the Sun sighted within a minute; confirm `SUN_SIDE_TOLERANCE_RAD` and record how often the iPhone flow is followed.
D5. **The phone's clock.** Compare each phone's clock with a time signal; the words assume it is right to well under a minute (Requirement 9.2).
D6. **Persistence.** Take a sight, lock the screen for an hour, switch apps, kill the browser; check what the Storage adapter returns on both phones, including private browsing.
D7. **Reading in the field.** Read the words and the map outdoors in full sun with one hand, on both phones.

## Open questions

Each question has a proposal. Decisions of the lead's review of 2026-11-20 are recorded with each question as "Decided:". The first group is for the lead; the second belongs to the owner.

### Questions for the lead

**N1. The Sun's position: no dependency, against `tech.md`.**

Decided (2026-11-20): the steering stands. `astronomy-engine` `2.1.19`, pinned, imported by the Sun wrapper alone; the wrapper is tested against the published values with the bounds of Requirement 3.5; its refraction is not used (Requirement 3).

**N2. Absolute altitude, against `math-conventions.md`.**

Decided (2026-11-20): the probe's `3.3° ± 0.5°` is an index error and a scatter. Calibration is the core of the release (Requirement 15); without it the Fix uses `UNCALIBRATED_ALTITUDE` and says so before the position (Requirement 9.3).

**N3. A calibration of the phone's altitude in this release.**

Decided (2026-11-20): yes, by Sun sights at a place the phone knows (Requirement 15). Its stability over time is Device check D1; `INDEX_ERROR_OLD_MS` is the age after which the words say so.

**N4. How to choose between two crossings.**

Decided (2026-11-20): the Sun side when the compass reading is usable; the fit with three or more sights when the cost margin holds; otherwise both places are shown and the user chooses, undone by one control (Requirement 6.7).

**N5. The shared error in the ellipse.**

Decided (2026-11-20): inside Nonius by sensitivity, Core unchanged (Requirement 7.2, 7.9); the lower bound of 90 % binds in every band, the upper bound of 99 % only with no Common sigma (Requirement 7.3).

**N6. The scenes' calibration.** Decided (2026-11-20): every scene has the iPhone's bias of `-3.3°`; scenes (a) to (c) carry their own Index error record, and scene (d) has none and shows the difference, including by measuring it (Requirement 13.2).

**N7. No automatic deletion.** The scope says the user deletes; a set left from another place would give a wrong Fix. Proposal: no expiry, the span of the set's times always shown, and the words of Requirement 12.4. The alternative is to ask "same place?" when a sight comes more than 12 hours after the last.

**N8. The single circle on the map.** With one sight, the Circle on a world map is the best picture of the method for a class. It crosses the antimeridian and nears the poles, which UI's map refuses to fit. Proposal: words only in this release (Requirement 9.7); the drawing in the second.

**N9. Degrees and minutes.** Navigators write `38°42.1′ N`. Proposal: decimal degrees as Vigia does, since the decimals rule already keeps the precision honest and the Fixes are whole degrees wide; nautical miles beside kilometres (Requirement 9.1).

**O2. Tiles.** Proposal: the same tile provider and build variables as Vigia; the owner confirms that its terms allow a second app on the same key or none, before a demo link is public.
